## Script

You've probably heard this claim. Harvard studies show that coffee is good for the heart. But let me explain what's actually going on. Yes, large observational studies from Harvard School of Public Health show that coffee drinkers tend to have better cardiovascular outcomes. But here's the key detail most people miss. These studies show correlation, not causation. In real life, this is what happens. People with strong digestion and a stronger cardiovascular system tolerate coffee just fine, so they keep drinking it. But people with weak stomachs often stop drinking coffee because it causes acid reflux, nausea, or digestive pain. And people with weaker hearts often experience palpitations, anxiety, or a racing heart, so they avoid coffee too. So over time, the data ends up looking like this. People who drink coffee have healthier hearts, when in reality, it's healthier stomachs and hearts that allow people to keep drinking coffee in the first place. From a traditional Chinese medicine perspective, coffee is still not considered a health food. It's roasted, bitter, heating, and drying. It overstimulates heart young and irritates the stomach, especially on an empty stomach. So the real question isn't, is coffee healthy? It's, can your stomach and heart handle it?
