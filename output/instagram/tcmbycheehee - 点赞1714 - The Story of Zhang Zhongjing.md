## Script

When COVID happened, many people thought the world was facing something completely new. But in Chinese medicine, epidemic disease is something that has been studied for more than 1,800 years. This is episode one of my new series, The People Who Created Chinese Medicine. Today, we're introducing one of the greatest physicians in Chinese history, Zhang Zhongjing. More than 1,800 years ago, China faced a time of war, chaos, and epidemic disease. People were getting sick, families were torn apart, and society was struggling. In the middle of this, there was a young man named Zhang Zhongjing, who would later become known as the Sage of Medicine in Chinese medicine. But his story began with a tragedy. Historical records say that many of his family members died from an epidemic disease. Some accounts say that more than 200 of his family members were affected, and most passed away within a short period of time. Imagine watching the people closest to you pass away one by one. That pain changed his life forever. From that moment on, Zhang Zhongjing dedicated his whole life to medicine. But there was one major problem. Back then, medical knowledge was scattered. Different doctors had different experiences. Some knew herbs, some knew theory, and some knew treatments from experience. But there was no clear clinical system that connected everything together. So Zhang Zhongjing studied the classics. He learned from many teachers. He read a lot of books. He observed patients, and he practiced medicine for many, many years. And eventually, he noticed something important. Illness doesn't appear randomly. There's patterns, stages, and it changes over time. And this became one of the most important breakthroughs in Chinese medicine. He organized disease progression into recognizable patterns, and he created a system called the Six Stages System, which is still being used today. He made connections between complex concepts and made it into one clinical framework. In Chinese medicine, we now call it 理法方药. Theory, method, formula, and medicine. And this framework changed everything. Because even when doctors do not know the exact modern diagnosis, they can observe the body's patterns and come up with a treatment direction. Cold or heat. Excess or deficiency. Interior or exterior. Yin or yang. Instead of asking what disease this is, Chinese medicine also asks what patterns are we seeing. And that is why Zhang Zhongjing's work became so important. His book, Shang Han Zha Bing Lun, later became the foundation for two major classics, Shang Han Lun and Jin Gui Yao Lue. And for centuries, these books have been studied by Chinese medicine doctors worldwide. His formulas are still being used today, his way of thinking still guides us, and his pattern-based thinking still influences how we understand illness today. To me, Zhang Zhongjing is not just a historical figure. He is one of my greatest role models. Because he turned personal tragedy into a system that helped generations of people. And that is why even after 1,800 years, he is still remembered as the sage of medicine. This is episode one of The People Who Created Chinese Medicine, and I'll see you in the next one.
