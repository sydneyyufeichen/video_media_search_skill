In China, most people eat at regular times, and here's why. If you're someone who overworks and skips meals, or you have irregular eating habits, you need to watch this. In traditional Chinese medicine, there's something called the organ clock. Your body runs on a daily rhythm, where different organs work better at certain times of the day. In the morning between 7 and 9, your stomach is at its strongest. That's why breakfast is meant to be your first meal, not coffee on an empty stomach. Around midday from 11 to 1, circulation and digestion are strong. Which is why lunch is when your body handles food most efficiently. In the early evening, roughly 5 to 7, your kidneys are most active. That's why dinner is meant to support recovery, not overload digestion. When you eat with this rhythm, digestion feels lighter, and your energy stays steady. But when you skip meals, eat randomly, or eat late at night, you slowly weaken your digestive fire. And that's when bloating and fatigue show up. And one last rule most people ignore, you don't need to eat until you're full. In Chinese medicine, stopping at about 80% full actually gives you more energy. Overeating makes digestion heavier and leaves you tired.
