## Script

As the weather gets colder, I want to share one of my favorite bedtime rituals from traditional Chinese medicine. It's simple, soothing, and just takes a bucket of warm water. Forget fancy tools. This is pure natural warmth for your whole body. In traditional Chinese medicine, your feet are connected to major meridians, especially the kidney, liver, and spleen channels. When your feet are cold, your yang energy sinks, circulation slows, and your whole system tightens. A hot foot bath helps with three major things. One, warms your kidney yang. Your kidneys act like your body's battery. Warming the feet helps protect and recharge your energy. Two, boosts circulation and relax your mind. The heat draws blood downward and releases tension. Your body naturally shifts into rest mode. Three, moves stuck qi and eases stress. If your mind races at night, soaking your feet helps ground that extra energy. Here's how I do it. Use comfortably hot water and cover your ankles. Soak for 10 to 15 minutes while you breathe and decompress. That's it. If you get cold hands and feet or struggle to wind down at night, try a hot foot bath. Simple, warm, and deeply restorative.
