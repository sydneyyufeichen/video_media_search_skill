In a previous video, I explained why Chinese medicine says the best time for cardio is between 5 and 7 a.m. And many of you asked about weightlifting. And the best time for that is between 3 and 5 p.m. Here's why. In traditional Chinese medicine, your body follows a 24-hour organ clock from 3 to 5 p.m. This is bladder time. When your circulation is strongest, your joints are looser, and your body can handle physical stress better. That makes it the ideal window for strength training. You're stronger, more flexible, and less likely to get injured. That's why many people feel their strongest in the late afternoon. But after 5 p.m., your body shifts into kidney time. In Chinese medicine, the kidneys store your body's deep energy reserves. At night, your body wants to restore energy, not burn it out. Heavy workouts too late can leave you feeling drained and even affect your sleep. And here's what most people don't realize. Traditional Chinese medicine doesn't promote extreme workouts. Practices like taiji and qigong use slow controlled movements to improve circulation, support your organs, and build energy instead of draining it. So the rule in Chinese medicine, lift between 3 and 5 p.m. and don't overdo it. Train with your body, not against it.
