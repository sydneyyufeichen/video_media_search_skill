In Chinese medicine, we have something called the organ clock. Over a 24-hour period, every two-hour window is associated with a different organ system being at its strongest. The idea is that if you eat according to this organ clock, you support better digestion and more steady energy throughout the day. From 7 to 9 a.m. is stomach time. According to Chinese medicine, this is the best time to eat breakfast because your stomach is believed to be at its strongest. Then comes 9 to 11 a.m., spleen time. In Chinese medicine, the spleen is responsible for transforming the food you eat into the energy your body uses. That's why these hours are associated with better mental clarity, focus, and steady energy. From 11 a.m. to 1 p.m. is heart time. According to Chinese medicine, there's plenty of qi and blood in the center of the body to support digestion, making it another ideal time to eat. The key is to eat when your digestive fire is naturally at its strongest. Another important principle is to avoid eating late at night. Nighttime is when your digestive system is supposed to rest and repair itself, not keep working. And one last thing. You don't need to eat until you're completely full. Instead, eat until you're about 70 to 80 percent full. According to Chinese medicine, overeating puts extra strain on your stomach and spleen, while eating just enough helps you digest more efficiently and leaves you feeling lighter and more energized.
