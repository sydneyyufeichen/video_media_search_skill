If you've ever eaten with a Chinese family, especially in northern China, you've probably noticed we eat a lot of garlic. Sometimes it's cooked into our food, sometimes it's pickled, other times we literally bite into a raw clove while eating. And very often, we'll crush raw garlic, mix it with Chinese vinegar to make a dipping sauce for dumplings and other foods. And it's not just because it tastes good. In traditional Chinese medicine, garlic is considered warm and pungent. It's traditionally used to support digestion, move qi, and help with food stagnation, especially when you're eating heavier, greasier foods. Vinegar has its own role too. In Chinese medicine, vinegar is associated with the liver. It's also commonly paired with rich foods because the acidity helps cut through the fattiness and makes the meal feel less heavy. So when you combine garlic and vinegar, you get this incredibly simple pairing that Chinese people have been eating for generations, alongside foods like dumplings, noodles, and meat. Modern research has also studied garlic for sulfur compounds like allicin and their potential cardiovascular and antimicrobial benefits, while vinegar's acidic acid has been studied for its effects on post-meal blood sugar. So that little bowl of garlic and vinegar on a Chinese dinner table, it's not just a dipping sauce. It's another example of a much bigger idea in Chinese culture, that food can be medicine.
