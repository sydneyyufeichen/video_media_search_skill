## Script

In Chinese culture, we say that cats are very good feng shui because they bring prosperity. That's why in Mandarin, we call them fa cai mao, the prosperity cat. And that's why you see that little gold cat waving its paw in Asian restaurants or Chinatown shops. It's placed right by the counter to attract money and keep good fortune flowing. But from a traditional Chinese medicine perspective, animals, especially cats, are extremely sensitive to the qi of a space. They can feel whether the environment is balanced, warm, stagnant, or off. So when a cat naturally loves your home or your garden, it usually means the qi is healthy and harmonious. That's considered very auspicious in feng shui. But if cats avoid your space, or worse, if cats keep getting sick or dying around your home, that's a major red flag. In feng shui, it means the energy is disrupted. In TCM, it can signal stagnant qi, excess dampness, or environmental imbalance that needs to be fixed. So yes, having happy cats around is a blessing. If not, it might be time to clear the space and reset the qi. Sometimes cats tell you everything.
