Do you really need to avoid all raw foods according to Traditional Chinese Medicine?

Not exactly. TCM is about eating for your body and the season—not following rigid food rules.

## Script

Wait, I thought traditional Chinese medicine says you're never supposed to eat anything raw. Actually, that's one of the biggest myths about TCM. TCM doesn't say raw food is bad. It says the right food depends on the person, the season, and the amount. Take summer for example. In China, people commonly eat tomatoes, cucumbers, watermelons, pears, peaches, and many other fruits raw. Why? Because according to Chinese medicine, these foods help cool excess heat and replenish fluids during hot weather. And here's something most people don't realize. Even in winter, some raw foods are still encouraged. Things like walnuts, almonds, and other nuts are often eaten raw because they're considered nourishing and energy-supporting. The issue isn't raw food itself. The issue is when someone with weak digestion is eating giant salads, smoothies, ice drinks, and raw vegetables every single day. So that's when you might start feeling bloated, tired, gassy, or have loose stools. So TCM isn't anti-raw food. It's anti one-size-fits-all nutrition. A cucumber might be perfect in the middle of summer. A warm bowl of soup might be better on a freezing winter day. Chinese medicine is less about food rules and more about eating according to the season and your body.
