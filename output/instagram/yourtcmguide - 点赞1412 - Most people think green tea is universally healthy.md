## Script

In Chinese medicine, there's a reason people in China and Japan almost never drink green tea iced. And they almost never drink it on an empty stomach either. But in America, green tea became iced matcha lattes, giant cold drinks, and healthy drinks people sip first thing in the morning. From a traditional Chinese medicine perspective, that's actually a huge difference, because green tea is considered bitter and cooling. And cooling sounds healthy, until you realize not everybody's body needs more cold. If you already have weak digestion, low energy, cold hands and feet, bloating, nausea, or poor circulation, too much green tea can actually make those symptoms worse. Especially iced green tea. That's why traditionally, in East Asia, green tea was usually consumed warm, with meals, and often more during spring and warmer seasons. Not as an iced cold drink first thing in the morning. And honestly, a lot of people notice this without realizing it. You drink green tea on an empty stomach, and suddenly you feel shaky, nauseous, and kind of unsettled. That's because for some people, it's simply too cold and too bitter for their system. So green tea isn't bad, but it's not universally good for everybody, especially the way we drink it in the West.
