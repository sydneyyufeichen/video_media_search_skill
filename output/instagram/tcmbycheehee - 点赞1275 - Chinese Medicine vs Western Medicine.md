## Script

There are pros and cons to both of the medicines. Personally, I like to look at the pros and the strengths of each one and see how we can care for the patient. Chinese medicine has a strength in pattern differentiation. So this is really what sets traditional medicine apart. Now when you look at western medicine, it's a lot of tests. It's a lot of scans, blood tests, trying to diagnose specifically what's the name of the illness. But what does Chinese medicine do when there's a new disease or like a new outbreak? Especially if there's a viral outbreak, sometimes there's no cure and you can only let the body do its job. But the thing that Chinese medicine can do when there's not yet a cure that's discovered, we can still support the body. Because instead of looking at the illness itself, we look at the pattern that the body is showing us. So if the body is showing a cold and damp pattern, right, and we see that the virus is thriving, then we use some warm and dry herbs to help balance out the situation. And when we shift the atmosphere in the body, we shift the environment, the body recovers. So that's the cool thing about Chinese medicine when there's an emergency, right, or when you need a specific diagnosis. Western medicine is definitely your go-to. For me personally, even when I was practicing, I would tell my patients, you have to go for a checkup. You have to find a diagnosis before coming here to make sure we know what diagnosis this is. And I see this as such a logical approach.
