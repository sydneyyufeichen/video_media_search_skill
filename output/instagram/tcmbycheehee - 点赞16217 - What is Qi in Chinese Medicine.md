What is Qi in Chinese Medicine? 

I’ve seen so many people mention “Qi” online but I was never satisfied with the way people defined it. After thinking and rewriting it many times, and making connections to modern science, this is my explanation of Qi.

## Script

Some people say qi as energy. Why don't we just say energy right away? Why do we say qi? Why is it not translated into English? Some things shouldn't be translated because when you translate it to one term, you have taken part of the definition away. So it's an inaccurate translation. I love to look at it from a physics point of view. The atom, it's the smallest particle before the subatomic particles were discovered. And when quantum physics and quantum mechanics came out, they found out that the smallest particle, it acts as a particle, so it's a physical matter, but it's also energetic. That's the easiest way for me to understand what qi means. It's the smallest matter in the universe that shapes everything we see, but it also has an energetic function. Depending on the functions, if it goes into the stomach, it's the wei qi. Stomach qi, okay, that helps us with our appetite. If it's under the skin, that's the defensive qi. So it's equivalent to what we call immunity. If it moves the blood in the meridians, that's what we call the yin qi. Wherever the qi goes, it has its own special name. So one more example is we can relate it to cellular respiration in science, right? We know that glucose and oxygen, when it combines, the whole process gives us energy. Similarly, thousands of years ago, Chinese medicine doctors have noticed that there's something called zhong qi. It's the central qi. It's right here in the middle in what we call dan zhong. How is this qi formed? This qi is a combination of two qis. The first qi is the fu qi, which is from the stomach. Once you digest the food, it transforms the usable parts, which is glucose. It takes those parts and brings it to the lungs, and it combines with the qi from the air. So when you look at the traditional Chinese character for qi, you can see that it is a qi at the top, a mi at the bottom, which is food. So there's food and there's air. And when both of them combine, that's how energy is produced in the body. So that's really how we can simplify Chinese medicine concepts and make it more accessible and easy to understand for everyone.
