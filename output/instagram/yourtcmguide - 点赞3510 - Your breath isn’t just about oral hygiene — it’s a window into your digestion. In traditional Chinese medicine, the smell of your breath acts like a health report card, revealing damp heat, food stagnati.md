## Script

Do you know why in Chinese medicine we say the smell of your breath is like a health report card? Because your breath doesn't just come from your mouth, it reflects what's happening deep inside your body, especially your digestion. If your breath smells strong, bitter, or foul, that usually points to damp heat in the stomach. Think greasy food, fried meals, sugar, alcohol. All of that creates heavy rotting heat that rises upward, and your breath is the first place it shows. If your breath smells sour, that's usually a sign of food stagnation. Food is sitting too long in the stomach, fermenting instead of being transformed. Over time, this can affect not only your breath, but also your mood, skin, and energy. And if you have chronic bad breath, even with good oral hygiene, that often means weak digestive fire. Your body doesn't have enough energy to fully cook the food, so toxins linger, and they escape through the breath. In Chinese medicine, we say the stomach is like a cooking pot. If the fire is too weak, food stays raw. If it's too hot and damp, food rots and ferments. Your breath is like the steam rising from that pot, telling exactly how digestion is going. So bad breath isn't random. It's your body sending you a signal. And in Chinese medicine, listening to those signals is the first step to real long-term health.
