## Script

In China, people almost never drink ice water, and if you ask for it at a restaurant, you'll probably shock the waiter. Here's why. In traditional Chinese medicine, your stomach is like a small fire. Every time you eat or drink, that fire has to cook the food to turn it into energy. And when that fire is hit with ice water, it's like dumping a bucket of cold water on glowing embers. The flame weakens right away. That's why after an ice drink, you might feel bloated, heavy, or even a little tired. But with warm water or tea, that flame remains steady. Your digestion works better, your body takes in nutrients more easily, and you actually feel more energized throughout the day. So while ice water feels refreshing in the moment, in China, it's avoided, because keeping your stomach warm is seen as keeping your whole body healthier. It's a small change, but over time, it can lead to better energy, smoother digestion, and less of that sluggish after meal crash. That's why you'll see people sipping warm tea all year round, even in the middle of summer.
