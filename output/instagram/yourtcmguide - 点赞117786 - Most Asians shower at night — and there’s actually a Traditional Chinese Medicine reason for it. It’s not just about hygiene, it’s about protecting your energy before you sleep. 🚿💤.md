## Script

Have you ever noticed that Asians usually shower at night while Americans shower in the morning? There's actually a traditional Chinese medicine reason for that, and it's not just about hygiene. In TCM, nighttime is when your body shifts to recovery mode. Your blood and energy return inward to repair your organs, kind of like your body's nightly maintenance cycle. So if you shower at night, you're literally washing off dust, pollution, and dampness from the day before you into rest mode. You're letting your body recharge in a clean, warm state, which supports your sleep, your skin, and even your immune system. But when you shower first thing in the morning, your pores are still closed and your body's energy hasn't fully woken up yet. That sudden water exposure, especially cold, can shock your system and cause fatigue or stiffness over time. So the next time you come home, think of your shower as more than just hygiene. It's a nightly ritual to reset your energy and protect your health. That's why in Chinese culture, we shower before bed, not after waking up.
