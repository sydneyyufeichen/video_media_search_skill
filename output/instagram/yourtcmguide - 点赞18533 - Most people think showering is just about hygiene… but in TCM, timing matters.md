## Script

Have you noticed that Asians usually shower at night while Americans shower in the morning? There's actually a traditional Chinese medicine reason for that, and it's not just about hygiene. In TCM, nighttime is when your body shifts into recovery mode. Your blood and energy move inward to repair your organs, like your body's nightly maintenance cycle. So when you shower at night, you're not just getting clean. You're washing off the dust, pollution, and dampness from the entire day, right before your body goes into rest mode. That means you're going to bed in a clean, warm state, which helps your sleep, your skin, and even your immune system. Now compare that to morning showers. When you first wake up, your body hasn't fully activated yet. Your circulation is still low, your pores are more closed, and sudden water exposure, especially cold, can shock your system. Over time, that can lead to fatigue, stiffness, or low energy. So the next time you get home, think of your shower as more than just hygiene. It's a reset for your body. That's why in Chinese culture, we shower before bed, not right after we wake up.
