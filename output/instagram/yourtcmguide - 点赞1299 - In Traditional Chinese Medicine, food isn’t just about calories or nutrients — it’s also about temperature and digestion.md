## Script

A lot of people think raw equals healthy, but in traditional Chinese medicine, it's not that simple. See, your stomach is like a cooking pot. Your digestive system is supposed to transform food into energy. And when you constantly throw cold, raw foods into that pot, it's like trying to cook with a fire turned off. That's why in Chinese culture, some foods are eaten raw, while others are almost always cooked. Things like cucumbers, watermelon, lettuce, pear, tomatoes, those are cooling foods. In moderation, especially hot weather, they're usually fine raw. But foods like broccoli, kale, mushrooms, carrots, and cabbage, they're usually cooked. Why? Because they're fibrous and harder to digest raw. Cooking softens them, warms the food, and makes them easier on your stomach. In TCM, warm cooked foods help support digestion and energy. Even garlic and ginger, which are warming foods, are usually cooked into meals instead of eaten raw. And this matters even more if you feel bloated after eating, have low energy, cold hands and feet, or feel tired after salads and smoothies. That doesn't mean raw foods are bad. TCM is all about balance. In the summer, more raw foods can help clear heat. But too much raw food, especially cold foods straight from the fridge, can weaken digestion over time.
