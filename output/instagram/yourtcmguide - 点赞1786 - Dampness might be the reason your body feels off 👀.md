## Script

In traditional Chinese medicine, a lot of health issues are linked to something called dampness. In Chinese, it's called 湿气, usually translated as dampness, but the literal meaning is closer to wet wind. But this isn't just about being wet, it's a condition inside your body. Think of it like internal humidity. When there's too much dampness, everything starts to slow down. And here's how it builds up. Eating too much cold or raw foods like salads, walking barefoot on cold floors, even going to bed with wet hair. In TCM, these allow cold and damp to enter the body. And once dampness builds up, it creates an environment where problems can develop more easily. Over time, if dampness lingers, it can develop into something called phlegm. Not just the mucus you cough up, but also what TCM calls invisible phlegm, something you can't see, but your body still has to deal with. In Chinese medicine, phlegm is considered a pathological substance, something your body is trying to clear. So when your body is holding on to dampness, it's not something to ignore. In TCM, it's something to clear.
