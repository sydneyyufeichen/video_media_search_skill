## Caption

Ice water might feel refreshing—but it could be slowing down your digestion.

In Chinese medicine, your stomach works like a fire that needs warmth to function properly.

Cold drinks can weaken that process, leaving you feeling bloated, tired, or heavy after meals.

Switching to warm water is a simple habit that can improve energy and digestion over time.

Try it for a few days and see how your body responds.

## Script

In China, people almost never drink ice water, and if you ask for it at a restaurant, you'll probably shock the waiter. Here's why. In Chinese medicine, your stomach is like a small fire. Every time you eat or drink, that fire has to cook your food to turn it into usable energy. Now imagine pouring ice water on that fire. It's like pouring cold water on glowing embers. The flame weakens instantly. That's why after cold drinks, you might feel bloated, heavy, or even tired. Some people even notice more cravings later, because your body didn't fully process the meal. It's not random. Your digestion just slowed down. And over time, constantly weakening that fire can make your system less efficient overall. But when you drink hot water or tea, that system stays steady. Food breaks down better, your body absorbs nutrients more efficiently, and your energy stays more stable throughout the day. So while ice water feels refreshing in the moment, it might be working against your body. That's why in China, people sip warm drinks year-round, even in the middle of summer. It's not about tradition. It's about protecting how your body functions. It's a small habit, but over time, it can lead to better digestion, more stable energy, and less of that heavy feeling after meals.
