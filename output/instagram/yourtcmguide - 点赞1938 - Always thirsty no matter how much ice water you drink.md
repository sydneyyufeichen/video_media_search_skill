## Script

The people who are the most dehydrated are often the ones drinking the most ice water. Sounds backwards, right? But according to traditional Chinese medicine, there's a reason for that. In Chinese medicine, constantly craving ice-cold drinks can be a sign that your body isn't managing fluids efficiently. You drink water, but you still feel thirsty. Your mouth stays dry, and you're constantly reaching for another drink. Why? Because in traditional Chinese medicine, excessive cold is thought to weaken your digestive system, which is responsible for transforming food and fluids into usable energy and hydration. It's kind of like pouring cold water onto a fire. The weaker that digestive fire becomes, the harder it is for your body to properly distribute and retain fluids. So you may find yourself drinking more and more, but never feeling fully satisfied. Instead of only focusing on how much water you're drinking, Chinese medicine also looks at foods that help hydrate your body from within. Number one, watermelon. One of the most popular summer foods in China because it's refreshing, hydrating, and cooling. Number two, cucumbers. They're packed with water and are traditionally used to help clear summer heat. Number three, pears. In Chinese medicine, pears are famous for nourishing fluids and helping relieve dryness, especially when the weather is hot or dry. So if you're constantly thirsty, no matter how much ice water you drink, try adding more water-rich foods into your diet. Sometimes staying hydrated isn't just about drinking more water. It's about helping your body use it.
